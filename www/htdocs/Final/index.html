TRANSPORT SAFETY · HOSPITALISED INJURIES

A national picture of road trauma, and the people behind the counts.

Hospitalised road injuries rose across 2011–2021. Pedal cyclist hospitalisations increased by 2,772 cases (+51.4%), accounting for 50.7% of the national net increase. Men and women aged 40–64 record the highest counts among age bands. These are hospitalisation counts, not measures of crash risk or cause.

What may shape exposure and outcomes for adults aged 40–64+
  • Mixed-traffic exposure: commuting and fitness cycling can put riders alongside motor vehicles.
  • Recovery and vulnerability: age-related changes in recovery and bone strength can make injuries more consequential.
  • Street design: busy corridors without physically separated cycle lanes leave riders exposed to traffic.

BITRE CSVs record hospitalisation counts, not direct crash mechanisms. These are contextual discussion points, not crash-cause fields in the dataset.

STEP 5 · SAFER STREETS & SAFETY TECHNOLOGY

Protected cycleways that work for every age

New Zealand’s Urban Cycleways Programme offers a useful model for investment in connected, physically separated routes. Protected lanes can reduce exposure to motor-vehicle conflicts for middle-aged and older riders, especially on busy corridors.

Where a safe route is not available, communities can support lower-impact hobbies for adults aged 40–64+, including swimming, walking and off-road trail riding. These options complement infrastructure investment; they do not replace it.

STEP 5 · SAFER STREETS & SAFETY TECHNOLOGY

Smart tools for safer everyday rides

  • Route choice: Strava and Komoot offer route planning with low-traffic or cycling-friendly options.
  • Vehicle awareness: Garmin Varia rear radar can alert a rider to vehicles approaching from behind.
  • Crash alerts: Apple Watch and Garmin devices can detect some hard falls and notify chosen contacts when configured.
  • Risk mapping: AI-assisted maps can combine road design, traffic speeds and reported incidents to flag higher-risk corridors.

These tools can support route decisions and alerts; they cannot replace separated infrastructure or guarantee crash prevention.

About the data, suppression and interpretation

Source and scope: BITRE Hospitalised Injury, September 2023, based on AIHW data, 2011–2021. The KNIME outputs retain road-traffic cases and use hospitalisation counts excluding deaths under the source definition. Records describe cases, not necessarily unique people.

How the comparisons work: Changes compare 2011 with the selected year. Percentage change is (comparison − baseline) / baseline × 100. The contribution card divides the focused group’s count change by the national net count change; it is not a share of all cases. The indexed chart divides each annual count by its own 2011 count × 100.

Counts and exposure: These are recorded counts, not rates per population, trip or kilometre. Changes may reflect population, travel exposure, reporting and other factors. They do not establish a change in crash risk or its cause.

Reporting breaks: Victoria’s series is not directly comparable with previous years from 2012; New South Wales’ from June 2017. Consider these jurisdiction-specific breaks when interpreting long-term changes.

Suppressed and absent records: Suppressed totals remain unknown, never zero. First Nations lines contain gaps at suppressed years; no changing mixture of published categories is plotted as a total. An absent combination is not automatically assigned zero.

Grouping and geography: Car driver, passenger and unknown-position records are combined as Car occupant; heavy-transport positions are combined similarly. State data describe jurisdiction of usual residence. Australia totals also include residence unrecorded or migratory/offshore, so jurisdiction totals need not equal National.

Age and sex: The source variable is sex. Labels are retained as supplied. “Intersex or indeterminate or missing” is not a separate estimate of intersex people; a missing count remains unknown.

First Nations: The supplied CSV includes First Nations people only. It cannot support a First Nations versus Non-Indigenous rate comparison. Interpretation should respect Indigenous data governance and source limitations.
