Rates are not headcounts.

Rates adjust for population size. Pew’s U.S. trend uses adults; the international map uses residents of all ages. Country data years also vary. Those rates should not be compared as if they were one identical dataset.

DESIGN REVIEW

Reading the map

Exercise 7.1 · Visualisation 2

What appears to be the purpose of the chart?

To show how incarceration rates vary by location and emphasize that the U.S. rate is high compared with other countries with available data.

What kind of chart is this?

A choropleth world map: countries are shaded according to rate ranges.

What data does it encode, and how?

It encodes incarceration rates per 100,000 people of any age. Country shapes are the marks; fill color is the channel, grouped into ordered rate bands. Labels call out selected country names and exact rates.

Are there interactive features?

Yes. Hover or focus a country for its rate. Choose a region or click a country to zoom in and see a country-by-country list. Click the water or choose World to zoom out. The tabs switch to the country table or the supplied U.S. correctional-status table.

What features help get its message across?

A graduated color legend, high-contrast country outlines, a clear unit, and a few direct labels make the geographic pattern readable. The U.S. label draws attention to the central comparison. Source-year differences and illustrative mock values are disclosed above.

Which design do you think is best and why?

For showing where rates are high, this map is strongest because geography is immediately visible. For precise ranking and comparing small differences, a sorted bar chart is better; no single design is best for every question.
