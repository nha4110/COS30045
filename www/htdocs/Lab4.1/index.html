A little house
at the end of the garden

A small study in SVG geometry. Every mark is drawn in the same viewBox coordinate space; switch on the guides to see how those numbers map onto the picture.

01 — The garden, drawn in vectors
rect · circle · ellipse polygon · polyline · path line · text · g all coordinates in viewBox units

02 — Reading the drawing

Coordinates, made visible.

The viewBox is 900 units wide by 650 high. Its origin sits at the upper-left; x increases to the right and y increases down. The grid is only a guide, not extra artwork.

  • circleSun: centre (758,92), radius 46.
  • polygonRoof: apex (446,115); base corners (250,280) and (642,280).
  • rectHouse: top-left (282,270), width 328, height 245.
  • g + translateWindows: group starts (320,315); second window translates 185 units.
  • ellipseTree shadow: centre (220,526), radii 64 × 19.
  • lineEave: from (250,280) to (642,280).
  • polylineRoof highlight: points (267,273) (446,125) (625,273).
  • pathGarden path: starts at (447,516) and curves to the bottom edge.
  • textCoordinate label: guide text begins at (30,25).

03 — One group, two windows

Shared styling. Separate position.

The parent group gives both windows the same fill and outline. Nested groups use local translations, so each window can be positioned without repeating its pane styling.

<g class="windows" transform="translate(320 315)">
  <g transform="translate(0 0)">...window...</g>
          <g transform="translate(185 0)">...window...</g>
</g>

Inspect the live SVG in your browser’s Elements panel to follow these groups and their child shapes in the DOM.
